What is diamond microdermabrasion?

Diamond microdermabrasion is a non-invasive exfoliating treatment that uses a handheld, diamond-tipped device to remove dead cells from the skin’s surface.

As the tip passes over the skin, controlled abrasion exfoliates the outermost surface. Suction collects the loosened cells and debris.

This is a superficial treatment. It differs from deeper resurfacing procedures and should not be expected to produce the same results.

02

A smoother, fresher-looking complexion.

By removing accumulated surface cells, microdermabrasion may help improve:

  • The appearance of a dull complexion.
  • Rough or uneven surface texture.
  • The appearance of some superficial dark marks.
  • Overall skin smoothness and radiance.

Results vary according to your skin and the concern being treated. Changes are generally modest and may be temporary.

Deep wrinkles, significant scarring and persistent pigmentation concerns may require a different approach. A consultation helps establish realistic expectations.

03

What happens after treatment?

Your skin may feel more sensitive

Temporary redness, mild swelling or sensitivity can occur. Your response depends on your skin and the intensity of the treatment.

Keep your aftercare gentle

Follow the skin-care instructions provided by your practitioner. Ask when to resume exfoliating products or other active ingredients rather than returning to them automatically.

Protect your skin from the sun

Sun protection is an important part of aftercare. Follow the guidance provided on sunscreen and limiting sun exposure.

Allow your skin time to recover

Microdermabrasion usually involves a short recovery period, but this does not mean that irritation is impossible. Contact your practitioner if symptoms are unexpected, persistent or worsening.

Further sessions should be planned around your skin’s recovery and response.

04

Is it right for your skin?

Before treatment, discuss your skin concerns, sensitivity, medication and recent procedures with your practitioner.

If you take or have recently taken isotretinoin, scar easily, or have a skin lesion that is changing or bleeding, seek advice from a dermatologist before considering treatment.
