How does
LED Light Therapy work?

Understanding light-based skin care, the role of different wavelengths, and realistic expectations.

From Be Nice Beauty · Fitzrovia, London

Published 28 January 2024 · Updated 7 October 2026

In this article

How it works Different wavelengths What to expect Is it right for you?

01

What is LED light therapy?

LED stands for light-emitting diode. In skin-care treatments, LED devices deliver selected wavelengths of light to the skin without needles or mechanical exfoliation.

Light interacts with light-sensitive molecules in the skin. Different wavelengths can produce different biological responses, which is why treatment devices use particular settings for particular purposes.

The wavelength, intensity, treatment duration and device all matter. The colour of the light alone does not establish how effective a treatment will be.

02

Different light, different purposes.

Red light

Red light is studied for skin rejuvenation, including improvements in the appearance of fine lines and skin texture. The extent of any benefit depends on the treatment and your individual response.

Blue light

Blue light is used in some acne treatment plans because it targets bacteria associated with acne. It may help with inflammatory pimples, but it does not treat every type of acne and is not a solution for blackheads, whiteheads, cysts or nodules.

Near-infrared light

Some devices also use near-infrared wavelengths, which are outside the visible spectrum. Their suitability and intended use should be explained in relation to the specific device.

03

What can you expect?

During the session

Your practitioner will position the device and explain the treatment. Follow the device-specific instructions on eye protection and tell your practitioner if you experience discomfort.

Results develop individually

LED may form part of a broader skin-care plan. Results are not guaranteed, and some approaches involve a series of treatments rather than a single appointment.

Session length and frequency should follow the device instructions and your treatment plan. More exposure does not automatically mean better results.

Non-invasive does not mean risk-free

Temporary irritation or discomfort can occur. Ask about the risks associated with the device and your skin, and report any unexpected reaction.

04

A plan suited to your skin.

Before treatment, tell your practitioner about your medical history, medication, sensitivity to light and recent skin procedures. Some medicines or conditions may affect suitability.

If you have persistent acne or a diagnosed skin condition, discuss treatment options with a dermatologist. LED should not replace prescribed care without medical advice.
